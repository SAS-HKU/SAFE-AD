\subsubsection{Naturalistic traffic replay}
The field-query interface was examined on recorded traffic before its use in
closed-loop decision making. At each sensing instant, the measured vehicle
states are transformed to an ego-local frame and encoded as source, transport,
diffusion, occupancy, and road-support channels. The resulting local field is
then mapped back to the recorded world pose for visualization. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:pinn-naturalistic-overlay}, this mapping follows the official
dataset calibration: metric world coordinates are converted to orthophoto
pixels using the recording-specific scale, and all vehicles are drawn from
their recorded bounding boxes at the same frame. Thus, the heatmap, roadway,
and traffic participants share one coordinate system rather than being
juxtaposed as independent local-grid images.

  traffic recordings. Each row shows the same recorded frame and vehicle
  configuration under the prospective numerical field (left) and the

  maximum integrated numerical risk among frames with a passenger or heavy

  The inD example is recording-disjoint; rounD and exiD are external

The overlay is an interpretability check, not an accuracy test. In particular,
the apparent extent or smoothness of a heatmap does not establish that one
backend produces safer or more human-compatible decisions. Construct validity
is therefore evaluated separately against the observed three-second future
occupancy envelope on recording-disjoint inD data. This target is computed
from future trajectories and uses neither the PDE source variables nor the RL
reward. The instantaneous source obtains AUROC/AUPRC values of
$0.8249/0.4167$, while the frozen PINN obtains $0.8657/0.5143$; the
recording-bootstrap paired gains are $0.0408$ ($95\%$ CI
$[0.0190,0.0576]$) and $0.0976$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0720,0.1207]$), respectively.
These results support predictive relevance to near-future traffic occupancy,
but do not interpret the field as a calibrated collision probability.

\subsubsection{Decision-level numerical-to-PINN substitution}
Practical comparison is conducted after descriptor extraction and policy
inference. For each scenario and traffic seed, one frozen field-observing
policy is executed twice, replacing only the prospective numerical query with
the PINN query. Paired episodes report collision, minimum time to collision,
corridor-risk exposure, progress, speed, jerk, imposed follower deceleration,
backward risk flux, and query latency. Confidence intervals are obtained by
paired bootstrap over traffic seeds; two-sided paired randomization tests are
adjusted by the Holm procedure across the reported scenario--metric
comparisons. Efficiency superiority is claimed only if progress increases
while the safety, comfort, and social-externality indicators remain
non-degraded.

The currently completed merge swap is best interpreted as a substitution
test, not as evidence that the PINN is intrinsically less conservative. Across
ten paired seeds, both backends produce zero collisions and equal episode
length. Mean progress changes from $585.533$ to $585.473$~m
($\Delta=-0.061$~m), mean speed changes by $-0.0074$~m/s, and mean absolute
jerk changes by $-2.52\times10^{-4}$~m/s$^3$. The complete online field query
is also slower for the present CPU deployment ($6.97$ versus $3.88$~ms), even
though the isolated GPU neural kernel is faster in the field-core benchmark.
Accordingly, the revised claim is that the tested PINN preserves merge-policy
behavior to practical tolerance. A stronger safety--efficiency claim requires
the matched highway, intersection, and roundabout specialist swaps and cannot
be inferred from field magnitude alone.

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment:}
\emph{How can the authors demonstrate that the learned field captures genuine
interaction risk rather than reproducing manually injected surrogate
variables?}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}
We agree that agreement with the hand-designed numerical teacher is not, by
itself, evidence of genuine interaction risk. We therefore separated teacher
fidelity from construct validity and added a recording-disjoint evaluation
whose target is generated directly from observed future trajectories. inD
recordings 01--05 are used for calibration and recordings 06--11 are held out.
At every evaluated frame, each candidate field is compared with the observed
vehicle-occupancy envelope over the following three seconds; this target uses
neither the PDE source terms nor the reinforcement-learning reward. Relative
to the instantaneous source, the frozen PINN increases AUROC from $0.8249$ to
$0.8657$ and AUPRC from $0.4167$ to $0.5143$. The paired improvements are
$0.0408$ (95\% CI $[0.0190,0.0576]$) and $0.0976$ (95\% CI
$[0.0720,0.1207]$), respectively. We have narrowed the manuscript claim to
predictive relevance for near-future occupancy rather than calibrated
collision probability. We also rebuilt the qualitative replay through the
official dataset coordinate transform (Fig.~\ref{fig:pinn-naturalistic-overlay});
the rounD and exiD scenes are explicitly identified as external qualitative
replays and are not pooled into the recording-disjoint result.

\noindent\textbf{Changes in the manuscript:}
We added the recording-disjoint future-occupancy test, clarified its independent
target and confidence-interval unit, and replaced the former local-grid montage
with orthophoto-calibrated naturalistic replays. Absolute-error maps and
fidelity statistics were removed from the qualitative figure.

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment:}
\emph{The reported gradient error may systematically mislead acceleration,
deceleration, or lane-change decisions, but the paper does not analyze how PINN
approximation error propagates into policy performance.}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}
We agree that a grid-level gradient error cannot establish policy robustness.
We therefore added two downstream tests. First, identical frozen states are
passed through the numerical and PINN descriptor backends and then through the
same policy. On ten merge seeds, the eight-dimensional descriptor RMSE is
$4.72\times10^{-3}$, the mean action-vector difference is
$6.68\times10^{-5}$, and no query yields
$\lVert\Delta u\rVert_2>0.05$. Second, the backend is replaced in actual paired
closed-loop episodes. Both merge backends remain collision-free and complete
the same number of steps, while progress differs by only $-0.061$~m over
approximately $586$~m. These results show that the observed approximation error
is strongly attenuated for the tested merge policy. We do not generalize this
finding to other topologies. The revised protocol therefore evaluates matched
scenario-specific policies on highway, merge, intersection, and roundabout,
and reports paired safety, efficiency, comfort, social-externality, and runtime
outcomes rather than inferring control quality from field error.

\noindent\textbf{Changes in the manuscript:}
The discussion now distinguishes field-level diagnostics, synchronized
descriptor-to-action propagation, and paired closed-loop outcomes. The merge
result is presented as behavior-preserving substitution, while the broader
claim is explicitly conditional on the completed four-scenario policy swap.
