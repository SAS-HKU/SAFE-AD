\subsubsection{Naturalistic scene conditioning}
The field surrogate is conditioned on measured traffic rather than on a fixed
scenario identifier.  At each sensing instant, vehicle trajectories are
transformed into an ego-local frame and used to construct the instantaneous
source, transport, diffusion, occupancy, and road-support channels supplied to
the context-conditioned physics-informed neural network (PINN).  Figure~\ref{fig:pinn-naturalistic-conditioning}
illustrates this common interface on an urban intersection from inD, a
roundabout from rounD, and a freeway merge/exit from exiD.  The inD recording is
disjoint from the calibration recordings.  The rounD and exiD panels are
reported only as external zero-shot diagnostics and are not pooled with the
recording-disjoint fidelity statistics.  This distinction prevents qualitative
cross-dataset examples from being interpreted as evidence of calibrated
cross-dataset accuracy.

\subsubsection{PINN fidelity and downstream error propagation}
Figure~\ref{fig:pinn-fidelity-propagation} separates three questions that were
previously conflated: whether the field anticipates an independently observed
traffic outcome, whether the PINN reproduces the numerical teacher and its
spatial gradients, and whether the remaining approximation error changes the
policy action.  On six held-out inD recordings, the PINN increases the area
under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUROC) from $0.8249$ for the
instantaneous source to $0.8657$, with a paired improvement of $0.0408$ and a
$95\%$ confidence interval (CI) of $[0.0190,0.0576]$.  The corresponding area
under the precision--recall curve (AUPRC) increases from $0.4167$ to $0.5143$
($\Delta=0.0976$, $95\%$ CI $[0.0720,0.1207]$).  These labels are obtained from
the observed three-second future-occupancy envelope and use neither the PDE
source variables nor the reinforcement-learning reward.  The result therefore
supports construct validity for near-future interaction occupancy, although it
does not equate the field with a calibrated collision probability.

Across held-out HighwayEnv seeds, field correlation, hotspot intersection over
union (IoU), and gradient angular error are respectively $(0.969,0.833,
9.1^{\circ})$ on the highway, $(0.977,0.854,7.4^{\circ})$ on the merge,
$(0.851,0.609,21.7^{\circ})$ at the intersection, and $(0.760,0.165,
33.5^{\circ})$ on the roundabout.  The roundabout therefore remains the most
difficult topology and is reported as a limitation rather than averaged away.
To determine whether field-level error propagates into control, we replayed
identical states through the numerical and PINN descriptor backends for ten
paired merge seeds.  The eight-dimensional descriptor root-mean-square error
was $4.72\times10^{-3}$, while the mean and mean 95th-percentile action
deviations were $6.68\times10^{-5}$ and $4.60\times10^{-4}$, respectively; no
query produced $\lVert\Delta u\rVert_2>0.05$.  In the subsequent closed-loop
backend swap, both backends produced zero collisions and the same mean episode
length, while mean route progress changed by only $-0.061$~m from a numerical
reference of $585.53$~m.  Thus, the tested merge policy is insensitive to the
observed surrogate error at the action level; this conclusion is not extended
to the weaker roundabout case without an equivalent closed-loop swap.

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment:}
\emph{How can the authors demonstrate that the learned field captures genuine
interaction risk rather than reproducing manually injected surrogate
variables?}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}
We agree that agreement with the hand-designed numerical teacher alone cannot
establish construct validity.  We therefore added a recording-disjoint test
whose target is generated directly from observed future trajectories and is
independent of both the PDE source terms and the reinforcement-learning reward.
Specifically, recordings 01--05 from inD are used for calibration, recordings
06--11 are held out, and each candidate field is evaluated against the observed
vehicle-occupancy envelope over the following three seconds.  The
instantaneous source obtains an AUROC of $0.8249$ and an AUPRC of $0.4167$,
whereas the context PINN obtains $0.8657$ and $0.5143$, respectively.  The
paired gains are $0.0408$ (95\% CI $[0.0190,0.0576]$) for AUROC and $0.0976$
(95\% CI $[0.0720,0.1207]$) for AUPRC.  The prospective numerical teacher
provides an upper-reference AUROC/AUPRC of $0.9096/0.5836$.  Consequently, the
PINN retains predictive information about where traffic will occupy the scene
beyond that available from the instantaneous injected source.  We have revised
the claim accordingly: the experiment supports near-future occupancy construct
validity, not a claim that the field is a calibrated probability of collision.
Figure~\ref{fig:pinn-fidelity-propagation}(e) and the accompanying text now
report the split, independent target, confidence intervals, and this narrower
interpretation.  We additionally show rounD and exiD as explicitly labelled
zero-shot diagnostics in Fig.~\ref{fig:pinn-naturalistic-conditioning}; because
these datasets were not used to calibrate the accepted checkpoint, they are not
pooled into the main quantitative result.

\noindent\textbf{Changes in the manuscript:}
We added the naturalistic-conditioning figure in the methodology section and a
new ``PINN fidelity and downstream error propagation'' subsection in the
experimental section.  The latter includes independent future-occupancy
validation and recording-level bootstrap confidence intervals.

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment:}
\emph{Table 2 reports gradient errors ranging from 0.067 to 0.096 across
datasets.  Since gradient descriptors inform longitudinal and lateral actions,
an error of approximately 10\% may systematically mislead the policy.  The
paper does not analyze how PINN error propagates through the descriptor pipeline
into policy performance.}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}
We agree that the previous normalized gradient-error column did not reveal the
directional or policy-level consequence of the approximation.  We replaced
that single quantity with field correlation, hotspot IoU, and the angular error
$\theta_{\nabla}$ between numerical and PINN gradients.  On held-out HighwayEnv
frames, $\theta_{\nabla}$ is $9.1^{\circ}$ for highway driving,
$7.4^{\circ}$ for merging, $21.7^{\circ}$ for intersection, and
$33.5^{\circ}$ for roundabout.  This analysis confirms that the approximation
is topology dependent and that roundabout querying remains the least accurate
case; the revised manuscript states this limitation explicitly.

More importantly, we added a direct error-propagation experiment instead of
inferring control reliability from grid error.  For ten paired merge seeds, the
same frozen states were evaluated with numerical-teacher and PINN descriptors
before passing both observations through the identical policy.  The mean
eight-dimensional descriptor RMSE is $4.72\times10^{-3}$, the mean action
deviation is $6.68\times10^{-5}$, and the mean 95th-percentile deviation is
$4.60\times10^{-4}$; the rate of
$\lVert\Delta u\rVert_2>0.05$ is zero.  We then performed an actual closed-loop
backend swap.  Both backends completed the paired episodes without collision
and with identical mean episode length; progress differs by $-0.061$~m from a
$585.53$~m numerical reference, and mean speed differs by $-0.0074$~m/s.  These
results show that, for the tested merge policy, the remaining field error is
strongly attenuated by descriptor extraction and does not cause a practically
meaningful action change.  We intentionally do not generalize this statement to
the roundabout, for which the gradient error is higher and an equivalent
policy-backend swap is still required.

\noindent\textbf{Changes in the manuscript:}
Table~2 now reports interpretable spatial and directional fidelity metrics, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:pinn-fidelity-propagation}(f)--(g) traces approximation error from
the field through the descriptors to the policy action.  The discussion narrows
the robustness claim to the tested merge backend swap and identifies
roundabout-gradient fidelity as a remaining limitation.
